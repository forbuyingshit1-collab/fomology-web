# FLUX.3 directing method

## Interview discipline

Ask one question at a time and only when the answer changes mode, story, timing, framing, continuity, or audio. Offer a recommended default. Stop interviewing once the brief is executable.

Resolve in this order: starting material and mode; viewer/platform/success; duration/ratio; subject/action/change/final image; camera/environment/continuity; audio/dialogue.

## Mental model

Prompt is not a spell or a pile of attractive words. It is a shooting brief that tells the model what the camera sees and hears, what changes, and the final image.

Build a scene spine:

```text
subject's immediate desire + physical obstacle/change + spatial relationship + viewer focal point + rhythm
```

Lock five anchors: governing emotion, recurring visual/audio motif, anchor object, irreversible change, and precise final image. Convert emotion into recordable facts such as gaze, grip, posture, pace, hesitation, environmental pressure, and sound.

## Shot design

For each beat use only what materially changes the result:

- time range when timing matters;
- framing and focal subject;
- one clear physical action and endpoint;
- one primary camera behavior and its motivation;
- environment and motivated light;
- concrete sound source;
- transition only when a cut occurs.

Baseline: one framing term + one movement term + one clear subject action. Keep geography readable and screen direction stable. For important action describe preparation → contact/change → reaction/recovery. Make VFX affect dust, water, cloth, light, surfaces, sound, or balance.

## Continuity

Lock only failure-prone details: exact character/object count, identity, costume/color, signature prop geometry and hand, scale, screen position, movement direction, and elements that must not change. Use explicit state transitions such as “begins fully closed, opens once, remains open.”

## Prompt forms

- One-line natural language for one continuous action/B-roll.
- Labeled fields when the user must tune separate levers.
- Timecoded timeline when action, cuts, dialogue, or effects must land in order.
- Full multi-shot schema only when identity/story must hold across several shots.

Do not maximize length. Remove adjective piles, repeated negatives, invisible micro-detail, and contradictory camera/optics.

## Audio

Speech needs a visible speaker or explicit voiceover, exact quoted words, language, register, pace, delivery, and social intention. Preserve exact Thai dialogue in Thai script. Ambience and effects must have plausible sources. Keep competing voices/music below key speech.

## Originality

When a request risks copying protected content, keep only high-level medium/genre traits and replace real names, logos, UI, character designs, slogans, jingles, presenter likenesses, and exact industrial design with original equivalents.
